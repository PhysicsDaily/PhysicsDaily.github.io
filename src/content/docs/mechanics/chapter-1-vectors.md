Many physical quantities need more than a number. To describe a displacement, velocity, force,
or acceleration, we must also say which way it points. These quantities are **vectors**.

## Scalars and vectors

A **scalar** has magnitude only. Mass, time, temperature, and energy are scalars.

A **vector** has magnitude and direction. We write a vector in bold, such as $\mathbf{v}$, or
draw it as an arrow. Its length represents magnitude and its arrowhead represents direction.

## Components

On a two-dimensional coordinate system, a vector can be expressed using horizontal and vertical
components:

$$
\mathbf{A} = A_x\mathbf{i} + A_y\mathbf{j}
$$

where $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are unit vectors along the $x$- and $y$-axes. The magnitude
of $\mathbf{A}$ is

$$
|\mathbf{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2}.
$$

Components let us analyze each direction independently, which is essential for kinematics and
Newton's laws.
